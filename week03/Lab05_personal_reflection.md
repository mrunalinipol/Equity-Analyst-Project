# Lab 05 Personal Reflection

Write this reflection in your own words.

## Insight line I trust least

Which Insight line do you trust least?

I trust the Investment Advisor's Insight line the least.


Why do you trust it less than the other two Insight lines?

The Investment Advisor's number depends on more judgment than the other

two. The Research Analyst and Financial Analyst usually cite one line

from a statement, like operating cash flow divided by net income or total

debt divided by equity, and I can find that line and check the math

myself. The Advisor's number is often a ratio built from several pieces,

such as dividends plus buybacks divided by operating cash flow, or debt

due within 12 months divided by cash. To check it, I have to find each

piece in the filing, and the cited line usually covers only one of them.

It also has to decide what counts as "due within 12 months," which two

readers could do differently.

The Advisor also writes about the long term, but the filing only reports

past results. So its Insight line can be a true number that still doesn't

fully support the forward-looking point it is making. The other two lines

make smaller claims, like "cash conversion was 0.7x," that the number

proves directly.


## Agent-team starting point

Which team member's agent team will your team use as its starting point?

We will start from my agent team, because its three roles read the

filings in clearly different orders (narrative first, statements first,

and business plus cash use) and each one produced the required format,

with a sourced Insight line as the last line. That gives us three views

that are easy to compare.


What does that agent team still lack?

It still lacks a check that the cited filing line really contains the

number in the Insight line. Right now the roles cite a source, but

nothing confirms it, which is the main reason I trust the Advisor's line

less. It also lacks a step that compares the three analysts and points

out where they disagree, and a clear rule for what to do when a number

is "not in evidence" so the scorecard doesn't end up with gaps.
